# Rules and submission

Last updated: 2026-11-15.

The official rules that shape what we build and how we submit it. This is a working summary; the full text of each guide is in [research/space-apps-guides/](../../research/space-apps-guides/KEY-FACTS.md), and [KEY-FACTS.md](../../research/space-apps-guides/KEY-FACTS.md) there names the guide behind every fact.

Source: the NASA Space Apps participant guides at https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/ , read on 2026-11-14.

## Deadline

Click "Submit for Judging" on the team's project page before **Sunday 15 November 2026, 11:59 p.m. Beirut time**. Anything saved after that is not judged. The page can be edited and resubmitted until the deadline; only the last saved version counts.

## Demo rules

- The demo is mandatory: either a **video of 30 seconds or less**, or a **slide deck of at most 7 slides** including the title slide.
- Going over either limit makes the project ineligible for global judging.
- It is hosted outside the Space Apps site, and the link must open with no login. Test it in a private browser window.
- It must be in English, or have English subtitles.
- Suggested order for the video, from the submission guide: who we are; the challenge and why we chose it; the project's title; how it works, shown with visuals and the NASA data used; the future impact.

## Rules about AI

- AI tools may be used to speed up the work. The guide says they should not be used to come up with the idea of how to solve the challenge.
- Images and video made with AI must carry a visible watermark.
- AI-made code, data and audio must be disclosed in the description and in metadata.
- AI-made content must not use or alter NASA logos, flags or mission insignia.

Our record is [reference/ai-use.md](../reference/ai-use.md).

## Other rules that affect the repository

- The project must respond to one official 2026 challenge.
- Work submitted becomes open source. If no licence is chosen it defaults to Apache 2.0; ours is Apache 2.0.
- Materials that are not NASA's must be freely available or under an open-source licence.
- No images, video, audio or likeness of anyone under 18. No personal information.
- The project page accepts only images (JPG, PNG or GIF, up to 10 MB each). Videos and documents are linked from elsewhere.
- All six team members must be registered and confirmed for the Beirut event and appear on the team's Members tab before submissions close.

## The link to the final project must be public

The repository is public: https://github.com/itani-jr-404/moonmaps. The live app is https://moonmaps.vercel.app/. The `local/` folder, which holds private files, is never committed.
