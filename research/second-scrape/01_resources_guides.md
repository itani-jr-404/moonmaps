ABOUT THE NASA SPACE APPS CHALLENGE
What are the dates for the NASA Space Apps Challenge?

The 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge will take place November 14-15, 2026.

What is the theme for the 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge?

Get ready to explore The Next Frontier! From your own backyard to the edges of the universe, discovery awaits at the NASA Space Apps Challenge. This is your opportunity to collaborate with a community of like-minded problem solvers to turn data into tools that benefit humanity and propel us to the Moon, Mars, and beyond.

Who should participate in the NASA Space Apps Challenge?

The NASA Space Apps Challenge offers opportunities for students, technologists, and space science enthusiasts to better understand how to utilize NASA open data to respond to today’s most pressing science and space exploration challenges. No matter your age, skill level, or professional background, when you participate in the NASA Space Apps Challenge, you join a global community that embraces collaboration to develop projects and tools that improve life on Earth and in space.

Does it cost anything to participate?

There is no cost to participate in the NASA Space Apps Challenge. If you are asked to pay money to participate in any aspect of a NASA Space Apps event, do not pay anything and please report to info@spaceappschallenge.org.

Can participants under the age of 18 participate? If so, how?

The NASA Space Apps Challenge is open to all ages! If under the age of 18, participants must be registered for the event by a parent or legal guardian and must be accompanied by a parent or legal guardian at all times during the event. Please note that Local Events may have additional requirements for persons under 18 to be eligible to participate in person. Check with your Local Lead for more information.

Additionally, participants under 18 should not:

Upload a photo of themselves as their Avatar or as part of their Team page
Use photo, video, voices, or likenesses of themselves or others under the age of 18 in their project demonstration or project submission.

Please carefully review the Participant Terms & Conditions as well as the Team Formation and Project Submission Guide for further guidance related to participants under 18.

Where does the NASA Space Apps Challenge take place?

The NASA Space Apps Challenge takes place at hundreds of events around the world. You may participate in an in-person or virtual Local Event near you or the virtual Universal Event if there is not an event near you.

Local Events are organized by Local Leads. Learn more about how to host a Local Event.

What should I do if I experience or witness discrimination, harassment, or any form of bullying during the hackathon or other NASA Space Apps activities?

We strive to ensure that NASA Space Apps is a place where all feel they belong, where all are comfortable being authentic, and where all are empowered to contribute to their fullest.

If you experience or witness discrimination, harassment, or any form of bullying during the NASA Space Apps Challenge, or have any other concerns, please contact your Local Lead and/or the NASA Space Apps Global Organizing (GO) Team at conduct@spaceappschallenge.org. The GO Team will take appropriate action, which may include warning or expelling offending participants from the event.

NAVIGATING YOUR HACKATHON JOURNEY
How do I participate in the NASA Space Apps Challenge?

To participate in the NASA Space Apps Challenge, you must:

Log in or create an account on our website.
Register for the NASA Space Apps Challenge starting on August 26, 2026.
Choose a Local Event near you or the virtual Universal Event.
Form or join a team to address one of the challenges. Team formation opens on September 17, 2026.
Join your Local Event on November 14-15, 2026. Look for their schedule and location details on the Local Event’s page.
Submit your project for judging according to the ending time of your Local Event.

How do I register?

Step 1: Access Your NASA Space Apps Account

Returning Participants: Log in to your existing NASA Space Apps account.

New Participants: Sign up for a NASA Space Apps account. Once you input your information, you will receive an email to verify your email address. You will verify your email by clicking the link in the message; afterwards you can log into the NASA Space Apps website.

Step 2: REGISTER FOR THE 2026 NASA SPACE APPS CHALLENGE & CHOOSE AN EVENT

Register: Registration for the 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge is open and will remain open through the end of the hackathon on November 15.

Complete the registration form on the 2026 Hackathon page, ensure that you complete all required questions and read the Terms & Conditions.

Choose a Local Event: We suggest that participants choose an in-person or virtual Local Event based on their physical location. The NASA Space Apps Challenge features hundreds of Local Events, allowing participants to find potential teammates located in the same community or time zone, or those who speak the same language.

Find a Local Event on the 2026 Local Events page. You can search by your location name or use the map to locate a Local Event near you. If there are no Local Events in your area, you can register for the virtual Universal Event.

If an event is at capacity, you can select to join the waitlist. If space becomes available, you will be added to the Local Event by the Local Lead. You can change your Local Event by clicking "Leave this Event" on the Local Event page you have chosen.

If there is not a Local Event near you, or you prefer to participate virtually, you can sign up for the Universal Event.

Step 3: Connect With Your Local Lead

Local Leads are the organizers of NASA Space Apps Challenge Local Events. They are available to answer questions and help connect you to potential teammates prior to the hackathon. Local Leads are the primary points of contact for your Local Event and will support your hackathon experience. You may find their contact information on your Local Event’s page. Please reach out to them as you prepare, and for specific details regarding your Local Event!

The NASA Space Apps GO Team will send updates about the hackathon with Space Apps Messages, you can check those from the menu under your profile icon. Additionally, your Local Lead may utilize Space Apps Messages to send updates about your Local Event.

What is the Universal Event?

The Universal Event is a virtual, asynchronous event that takes place November 14-15. Participants can create and join teams just like in Local Events, and registration works the same. Projects from the Universal Event are judged during the same overall judging period as all Local Event projects; however, Universal Event projects are evaluated only in comparison with other Universal Event submissions. The selection rate for Global Nominees is comparable across both the Universal Event and Local Events, reflecting an equally competitive nature.

However, we highly encourage participants to join a Local Event near them if they have the option rather than the Universal Event. Local Events provide access to a closer community, coaching opportunities, and many are in-person. You can find all available Local Events on the Local Events page, and you can change your Local Event at any time.

Universal Event Teams

You can form or join a team with anyone else registered for the Universal Event. For step-by-step instructions on how to join or form a team, check out the Team Formation Guide. In order to be eligible for global judging, a Universal Event Team must have at least two (2) participants. This rule only applies to the Universal Event.

Starting on November 3, use Space Apps Connect’s Find a Team category to connect with potential teammates. Space Apps Connect is the engagement platform where you can communicate with other participants, subject matter experts, local leads, global collaborators, and the Space Apps Global Organizing (GO) Team about everything related to Space Apps.

Throughout your hackathon journey, you will receive communications from the NASA Space Apps GO Team about updates and actions you need to take on spaceappschallenge.org as part of the hackathon. You will also receive information from your Local Lead about information specific to your Local Event.

Each year we provide Participant Guides to help you navigate each step of the hackathon. What are the challenges?

Challenges are authored by NASA and Space Agency Partner subject matter experts and encompass a wide variety of Earth and space science topics. Each team must select a challenge to address during the hackathon.

A summary of each challenge will be released on September 17, followed by challenge statements on October 28, which include the full details and requirements. Resources for each challenge will be published on November 13. Teams are not allowed to begin working on the challenges prior to the hackathon.

Do I need to be on a team? When can I join a team?

Yes, part of the NASA Space Apps Challenge experience is connecting with like-minded problem solvers from your own community and around the world! Participants are encouraged to create teams that include individuals with a variety of skill sets and backgrounds. Being on a team provides you with the opportunity to learn from one another and sets your team up for hackathon success.

Local Event teams must have at least one (1) person and Universal Event teams must have at least two (2) people to be eligible for global judging. Teams may have no more than six (6) members and must be signed up for the same Local Event or the Universal Event. You may only be on one team and teams are only permitted to work on one challenge. We highly recommend that you work with other participants.

Team formation for the 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge will open on September 17. Once team formation is open, you can join or create a team. Step-by-step instructions on how to create or join a team will be provided in the Team Formation Guide, which will become available on September 17.

Step-by-step instructions on how to create or join a team are provided in the Team Formation Guide.

How do teams submit their project?

In order to be eligible for Global Judging, teams must submit their project through their team’s project page on spaceappschallenge.org. Step-by-step instructions on how to submit your project will be provided in the Project Submission Guide, which will become available on November 13.

What is Super Intelligence? Can teams utilize it during the hackathon?

In accordance with the September 29, 2026 U.S. Executive Order, "the executive branch shall use the terms 'Super Intelligence' and 'SI' in place of 'Artificial Intelligence' and 'AI.'"

Super Intelligence (SI) refers to computer systems that perform tasks typically associated with human reasoning, decision-making, content creation, and problem-solving. Examples include large language models, conversational assistants, image and video generation tools, foundation models, and code generation tools.

Participants may use SI tools to accelerate innovation, streamline development, and enhance creativity in support of their challenge solution. Each team's project submission must include a description of how SI tools were used during the development of their project.

What is Space Apps Connect?

Space Apps Connect serves as the collaborative communication forum for the NASA Space Apps Challenge community. It is completely FREE for registered participants to use. Joining Space Apps Connect is optional but highly encouraged. The platform allows you to connect with other hackathon participants as well as with Local Leads, Navigators, SMEs, Global Collaborators, and the NASA Space Apps Global Organizing (GO) Team. Space Apps Connect will open on November 3 and close at the conclusion of the hackathon.

Step-by-step instructions on how to navigate this platform will be provided in the Space Apps Connect Guide, which will become available on November 3.

Recognition and Awards

All NASA Space Apps Challenge participants will receive a certificate on their account’s profile page recognizing their participation upon submitting their project. Projects will then be judged on specific criteria to determine Honorable Mentions, Global Finalists, and Global Winners. Global Winners are announced upon the conclusion of judging. Global Winners will be invited to attend a virtual Winner’s Celebration in early 2027.

Learn more about this process in the Judging & Awards Guide.

HOW TO SEARCH SPACE APPS CONNECT

Space Apps Connect Etiquette: Before creating a post, please search for similar discussions. This helps prevent duplicate questions and reduces creating an unnecessary discussion post when a similar discussion already exists. Please read “How to Search for a Discussion” below before creating your post to familiarize yourself with how to use the filters. When writing the title of your post, make sure that it is clear, concise, and summarizes what you are posting. This will help the GO Team identify which labels to attach to your post and will allow others to find and respond to your posts as quickly as possible.

How to Search for a Discussion: If you have a question for the Space Apps Community, first search all of the discussion posts to see if someone else has asked the same question. To search:

1. Click on the “View all discussions” above the category names on the left-hand side.

2. Select the “Filter” button and select “All." This allows you to see all discussions regardless of their status (open, closed, locked, unlocked, answered, unanswered).

3. Click on the label filter to select labels you think would apply to the discussion you are looking for. You can also use the search bar within the label filter to find a specific label. For example, for a question related to the “Flame in Freefall” challenge, select the “Flame in Freefall” label.

You can select multiple labels. When you select multiple labels, only the discussions that contain all of the labels you select will appear in the results. If you select too many labels, there may be no discussions that match your query.

If you want to remove a label you have selected, go back to the label filter and click on the label you want to remove. For example, if you decide that you do not want to filter by the “Flame in Freefall” label, then go back to the label filter and deselect the “Flame in Freefall” label. You can also click on “View All Discussions” in the category section to remove all of the filters you have applied.

HOW TO POST ON SPACE APPS CONNECT

On the left-hand side of the Discussions section, you will see all of the Space Apps Connect categories. The only category you cannot post in is the “Announcements” category.

The content of your post will determine which category it should go in. For example, if you have a question about a certain challenge, you would want to post that question as a discussion under the “Challenges” category. More detailed descriptions of the Categories can be found above.

You can start a new post from the “All Discussions” page or the category that you’d like to post in directly.

1. Click on the “New discussion” button on the top right of the screen.

2. If you are starting on the “All Discussions” page, a page will open asking you to select the discussion category. Select “Get started” next to the appropriate category.

3. Once you add a title and your content, click the green “Start discussion” button to post it to GitHub Discussions.

4. If you want to move your discussion into another category, before or after you post, you can select the “choose a different category” above the Discussion title section and select a new category.

How to Comment on a Discussion: You can join a discussion by clicking on the post you would like to comment on, then enter your response into the box at the bottom of the page and click “Comment.”

How to Reference Another Discussion or User: GitHub allows you to reference other discussions and mention other users in your comments and posts. To reference another discussion:

1. Click on the word bubble icon in the content section of your discussion/reply/comment.

2. Start typing out the title of the discussion. Once the correct discussion you are referencing pops up, click it, and it will add the discussion reference number to your comment. In the below example, the User is referencing a discussion that is titled “Check out the Participant FAQ!”

Once you have selected the correct discussion you want to reference and are satisfied with your post, select the green button in the bottom right of the content section. The green button could be named “Comment,” “Start discussion," or “Reply” depending on the type of post you are creating. The reference number in your discussion will be clickable and point back to the referenced discussion.

3. If you want to mention another user in your discussion, type “@” followed by the user’s GitHub username. Please be courteous and refrain from spamming usernames.

PROJECT SUBMISSION START & END TIMES

Project submission opens on Saturday, November 14 at 9:00 a.m. in accordance with the time zone of your team’s Local Event. At that time, your team will be able to edit your team’s Project page and submit your project. Project submission closes on Sunday, November 15 at 11:59 p.m. in accordance with the time zone of your team’s Local Event.

Exceptions: If your team is registered for the Universal Event, Project Submission opens Saturday, November 14 at 9:00 a.m. and closes Sunday, November 15 at 11:59 p.m. in accordance with the local time of your Team Owner's area of residence.

Check with your Local Lead about times for your Local Event’s local judging and awards which may differ from the global submission times.

EDITING YOUR PROJECT SUBMISSION PAGE

Once you create or join a team, you will be able to edit your team’s Project Submission page. The Project Submission page is where your team will provide all of the details about your project. This is the page that the judges will review. The more information you provide on this page, the better the judges can rate your submission!

How to Access Your Team’s Project Page

Click on your profile icon on the top right-hand side of the NASA Space Apps website menu.
Click on “My Team.”
On your team page, select the “Project” tab. Note: The “Project” tab will appear only when the hackathon begins on November 14, 2026 at 9:00 a.m. local time.

How to Edit Your Project Page

Click the blue “Edit” button.
Save your project often so you do not lose your work! You can save your work as many times as you like before the end of the hackathon by clicking the blue “Save Progress” button on the right-hand side of the page. Note that your changes will only be viewable to your team until you submit your project for judging.
To prevent multiple team members from editing the project page at the same time, toggle the edit "Lock" to 'ON'. This will let other team members know you are making changes to the "Project" tab. However, be aware that another team member can still remove the lock and make changes to the project, overwriting any changes you have not yet published. So, stay in close communication with your team members when editing.

Sharing Files to Your Project Submission Page

The Project page DOES allow for the upload or copy/paste of images (JPG, PNG, GIF up to 10MB). However, it DOES NOT allow for the upload of videos or documents.
Including Videos or Documents in Your Project Submission: Upload your files to a cloud service or repository (e.g., YouTube, Google Drive, GitHub, OneDrive, Dropbox, etc.) and share a publicly accessible link with no logins, permissions, or registrations required. If judges can’t access your project, it can’t be evaluated or judged.
Most platforms have a “share” option that provides a URL.
Before submitting:
Set the file to public access
Test the link in a private browser or with teammates to confirm it works.
You’re welcome to link to a slide deck, video, or other visual aids. For additional tools and resources, see the Global Collaborators page.

Display an image of the project
Describe the user experience
Include how NASA and space agency open and space based data and technology make your solution possible

Team name
Names of team members
The challenge your team selected

JUDGING CRITERIA

At every level of judging, from Local and Universal Events to Executive Judging, your project will be evaluated on a scale of 1-to-5, in each of the following criteria:

Impact

This project makes an impact by addressing either a minor or major problem, which influences the quality and quantity of its overall effect. The extent to which it inspires or helps others varies, ranging from a broad audience to a select few.

Score of 5 (Outstanding Impact): This project demonstrates exceptional impact, addressing a significant problem with proposed solutions. Its reach is extensive, inspiring, benefits a large audience, or could change the world. The quality and effectiveness of the project far exceeds expectations, setting a high standard for others.

Creativity

This project demonstrates originality and innovation. It introduces a novel concept that has not been attempted before or presents an improvement upon existing ideas in a meaningful way. This project is distinguished by its level of creativity, showcasing a fresh perspective or inventive execution.

Score of 5 (Highly Creative and Innovative): This project showcases remarkable creativity, introducing a groundbreaking approach that has never been attempted before. It challenges conventional methods, presenting an original and inventive proposed solution that stands out in its field. The uniqueness of the idea makes it a true innovation.

Validity

This project presents a proposed solution that is grounded in scientific principles and demonstrates technical, functional, or logical feasibility. It is designed to effectively achieve its intended purpose and has the potential to function in real-world applications. The completeness of the proposed solution varies—it may be fully developed or still require significant refinement. Additionally, the usability and user-friendliness of the project influence its practicality and accessibility.

Score of 5 (Highly Valid and Feasible): This proposed solution is scientifically sound and technically feasible, demonstrating strong alignment with established principles. It is fully developed and ready for real-world implementation, effectively achieving its intended purpose. The project is highly usable and user-friendly, making it accessible and practical for widespread application.

Relevance

This project fully and directly addresses the challenge for which it was submitted, demonstrating a strong alignment with the intended objectives. It meaningfully integrates NASA data, utilizing it as a core component of the proposed solution rather than a superficial addition. The depth of engagement with the data enhances the project's credibility and effectiveness.

Score of 5 (Highly Relevant and Strong NASA Data Integration): This project thoroughly engages with the challenge, delivering a proposed solution that is deeply connected to the intended goals. NASA data is integral to the project’s success, providing meaningful insights that drive the innovation forward. The use of data is well-executed, enhancing both the credibility and impact of the solution.

Presentation

The team effectively communicated their project, clearly conveying the challenge, their proposed solution, and its significance. The storytelling was engaging and well-structured, ensuring the audience understood the project's purpose and impact. The clarity, organization, and delivery of their presentation played a key role in making their ideas compelling and clear. Judges evaluate the demo and full project to compute this score.

Score of 5 (Exceptional Communication and Storytelling): The team delivered a well-structured and compelling presentation, clearly articulating the challenge, proposed solution, and importance of the project. Their storytelling was engaging, allowing the audience to fully grasp the significance and potential impact of the work. The delivery was polished, structured, and highly effective in communicating their ideas.

While many NASA Space Apps Challenge teams consider the first four judging criteria carefully and excel at developing projects with great potential, remember to pay equal attention to the final criterion: presentation. We recommend devoting time and resources to developing your pitch and building a great story around your project. Be sure to recruit team members with a variety of skills, including expertise in areas like storytelling and design, to help document your project and share the value of your proposed solution with the world. For tips on how to craft an effective project demo, read the Project Submission Guide.

Best Storytelling Award: The project that most creatively communicates the potential of open data through the art of storytelling.
Global Connection Award: The project that best connects people around the world through technology.
Art & Technology Award: The project that most effectively combines technical and creative skills.
Local Impact Award: The project that demonstrates the greatest potential for making a difference in their local community.

ABOUT TEAMS

Teams are an important part of the NASA Space Apps Challenge as they are required for project submission and allow you to connect with like-minded problem solvers from your own community and around the world! Participants are encouraged to create teams that include individuals with varied skill sets and backgrounds. An assorted team provides you with the opportunity to learn from one another and sets your team up for hackathon success.

Do I need a team to participate?

Yes. While a participant is allowed to create a team and submit a project independently, we encourage all participants to be part of an actual team. We have found that the most successful projects come from teams of individuals with a variety of skills and backgrounds. For example, we encourage participants with non-technical backgrounds to form a team with developers, designers, and people with scientific backgrounds.

How many members can be on a team?

Local Event teams must have at least one (1) person and Universal Event teams must have at least two (2) people to be eligible for global judging. Teams may have no more than six (6) members and must be signed up for the same Local Event or the Universal Event. The ideal size for a team is four to five people to balance skill sets.

Can I create or join more than one team?

No. You may only be on one team and each team may only submit a project for one challenge.

If you wish to join a new or different team, you will first need to leave your current team by selecting the “Leave Team” button on your Team Member page.

If you wish to work on a different challenge, you may update to a new challenge by editing your team.

When can I form or join a team?

You will be able to officially join or create a team on the NASA Space Apps website on September 17, 2026. You may also wait to form your team until the hackathon. You may add members to your team until the hackathon ends on November 15, 2026 (11:59 p.m. local time).

Does it matter which team member officially creates the team?

Yes. One person from each team is responsible for creating the team. That person is known as the “Team Owner.” Please note that the Team Owner’s Local Event will represent the team and will appear on the respective Local Event page. Can people start working on the challenges as soon as they are published?

We appreciate the enthusiasm of participants who want to start as early as possible. However, participants should NOT begin working on their project until the hackathon begins on November 14, 2026 (9:00 a.m. local time) to ensure a fair and equitable process.

Since the NASA Space Apps Challenge is held over the course of only two days, participants are not expected to submit a fully developed project at the end.

What if I have questions about one of the challenges or datasets?

Questions about challenges or supporting resources can be asked in Space Apps Connect in the week prior to and during the hackathon. NASA Space Apps Navigators and NASA subject matter experts will be available to answer your challenge and data-related questions.

For information on how to access Space Apps Connect, please read our Space Apps Connect Guide.

Is it mandatory to pick one of the challenges?

To be eligible for Global Judging and Global Awards, teams must respond to one of the challenges.

Can our team work on more than one challenge?

No, teams are only permitted to work on one challenge.

My team has decided to tackle a different challenge. However, we’ve already created our team page. What should we do?

If you’ve already created a team but have changed your mind about which challenge you want to tackle, you are welcome to pick a new challenge. The Team Owner can do this by choosing the blue “Edit Details” option on your team's About page.
