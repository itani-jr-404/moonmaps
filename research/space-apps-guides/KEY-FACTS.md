## What to submit (project page fields)

Every required field must be filled in, or the submit button will not work (submission-guide.md):

1. **Project Name**
2. **High-Level Project Summary**: what you built, how it addresses the challenge, and why it matters.
3. **Link to Project Demo**: a public link (see the demo rules below).
4. **Link to Final Project**: a public link to a website, repository, drive, or similar.
5. **Detailed Project Description**: what it does, how it works, its benefits, your goals, and the tools, languages, hardware and software used.
6. **Use of Super Intelligence (SI)**: whether and how SI or AI tools were used. Judges do not see this answer, but they do see the SI markers inside the project.
7. **NASA Data**: the specific NASA data and resources used. **At least one NASA data source or resource is required to be eligible for a Global Award.**
8. **Space Agency Partner Data and Other References**: every dataset, resource, tool, piece of code, text and image used, including open-source ones. Copyrighted material needs permission.

Before submitting, tick two boxes: the Terms and Conditions box, and the "Confirm All Work is Original" box (submission-guide.md, judging-guide.md). You can edit and resubmit until the deadline. Only the last saved submission is judged (submission-guide.md).

## Demo rules

- The demo is mandatory. Choose one format: a **video of 30 seconds or less**, or a **slide deck of at most 7 slides**, counting the title and intro slides (submission-guide.md).
- **Going over 30 seconds or 7 slides makes the project ineligible for Global Judging** (submission-guide.md).
- Host the demo outside the site (for example YouTube, Google Drive or GitHub). The link must be public, with no login, permission request or registration. Test it in a private browser window (submission-guide.md).
- Everything must be in English. A video may be spoken in another language, but it must have English subtitles. Slides must be in English (submission-guide.md).
- The guide's suggested video timeline (submission-guide.md):
  - 0:00 — names of the presenters and the team.
  - 0:02 — the challenge you chose and why.
  - 0:05 — the project title and tagline.
  - 0:10 — how it works, shown with visuals, the user experience, and the NASA or partner data used.
  - 0:20–0:30 — the future impact.
- A slide deck must include the team name, the member names and the chosen challenge (submission-guide.md).

## File and link rules

- The project page accepts only images: JPG, PNG or GIF, up to 10 MB each. It does not accept video or document uploads. Link those from a cloud service instead (submission-guide.md).
- If you host your own website, the guide recommends (but does not require) requesting a FortiGuard Web Filter classification, so the judges' networks do not block it: https://www.fortiguard.com/faq/wfratingsubmit. Enter "N/A" as the company name (submission-guide.md).
- No images, video, audio or likeness of anyone under 18 (submission-guide.md, terms-and-conditions.md).
- No personally identifiable information (terms-and-conditions.md).

## SI (AI) usage rules

- SI tools are allowed to speed up the work. The guide says SI "should not be used to (come up with your idea of how to solve the challenge)" (submission-guide.md).
- **Images and video made with SI must carry a visible watermark.** SI-generated audio, code and data must be disclosed in the descriptive text and metadata (submission-guide.md).
- SI-generated content must not use or modify NASA branding, such as logos, flags or mission identifiers (submission-guide.md).
- Judges evaluate originality, intent and execution based on the SI-assisted elements you disclose (submission-guide.md).

## Judging criteria

Each project is scored from 1 to 5 on each of five criteria, at every judging level. The guide gives no weights, so treat all five as equal (judging-guide.md):

| Criterion | What earns a 5 |
| --- | --- |
| **Impact** | Addresses a significant problem, reaches a broad audience, and could change the world. |
| **Creativity** | A novel, groundbreaking approach that has not been attempted before. |
| **Validity** | Scientifically sound, technically feasible, fully developed, usable and user-friendly. |
| **Relevance** | Fully addresses the chosen challenge, with NASA data integral to it rather than superficial. |
| **Presentation** | Clear, polished, engaging storytelling. Judges score the demo and the full project together. |

The guide explicitly tells teams to give Presentation equal attention, and says a strong demo raises the Presentation score (judging-guide.md, submission-guide.md).

## How judging works

1. **Local judging:** the Local Lead's judges evaluate every Beirut submission and choose the Global Nominees. Local awards are separate from global awards (judging-guide.md).
2. **Global judging:** expert judges rank the nominees and pick the Global Finalists and Honorable Mentions (judging-guide.md).
3. **Executive judging:** NASA and Space Agency Partner executives award 10 Global Awards. These are Best Use of Science, Best Use of Data, Best Use of Technology, Galactic Impact, Best Mission Concept, Most Inspirational, Best Storytelling, Global Connection, Art & Technology, and Local Impact (judging-guide.md, awards.md).

## Eligibility rules

- The project must respond to one official 2026 challenge (judging-guide.md, terms-and-conditions.md).
- Every member must be registered and confirmed, not waitlisted, at the same Local Event, and must appear on the team's Members tab. The team's event is the Team Owner's event (judging-guide.md, team-formation-guide.md).
- Teams with China or Chinese-owned entity affiliations are reviewed under NASA's China funding restriction. This is about affiliation, not nationality (judging-guide.md, terms-and-conditions.md).
- Participants under 18 must be registered by a parent or guardian and accompanied at all times (faqs.md, terms-and-conditions.md).
- Participation is free. Report anyone who asks for money to info@spaceappschallenge.org (faqs.md).
- Original work you submit becomes open source under an OSI license. If you don't choose one, it defaults to **Apache 2.0**. Any non-NASA materials you use must also be freely available or OSI-licensed (terms-and-conditions.md).

## Team rules

- Local Event teams need at least 1 member. Universal Event teams need at least 2. The maximum is **6** members, and 4–5 is recommended (team-formation-guide.md, faqs.md).
- Each person can be on one team only, and each team can work on one challenge only. The Team Owner can change the challenge using "Edit Details" (team-formation-guide.md).
- The Team Owner approves join requests, must post team contact information on the About tab, and is the only person who can transfer ownership (team-formation-guide.md).
- Deleting a team erases its Project and Details content (team-formation-guide.md).
- Teams may not start working on challenges before the hackathon (faqs.md).
