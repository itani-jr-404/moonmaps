# Lunar south pole missions: background

Last updated: 2026-11-15.

Background as stated by NASA and partner agency pages. Plans change.

## Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO), NASA

LRO launched on June 18, 2009. NASA calls it the longest-lived lunar orbiting mission. Its data helps choose safe landing places and find areas near the south pole with water and extended sunlight [1]. One of its seven instruments, the Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter (LOLA), measures the Moon's elevation [1]. Moon Maps' terrain horizons come from LOLA grids.

## LCROSS, NASA

The Lunar Crater Observation and Sensing Satellite launched with LRO on June 18, 2009. Its goal was to find out whether water ice exists in a permanently shadowed crater at the south pole [2]. On October 9, 2009, its Centaur upper stage hit Cabeus crater at 11:31 UT, and LCROSS flew through the plume before it also impacted. LCROSS and LRO found evidence that the soil in shadowed craters is rich in useful materials, and scientists confirmed that the water was mostly pure ice crystals in some places [2].

## Chandrayaan-3, ISRO (India)

Chandrayaan-3 was a lander and rover mission to show safe, soft landing and roving on the Moon, with a planned life of one lunar day (about 14 Earth days) [3]. ISRO announced that it soft-landed on August 23, 2023 [4]. LROC places the lander at 69.3741 S, 32.32 E, about 600 km from the south pole [5]. Moon Maps uses the LROC position.

## Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS), NASA

Through CLPS, NASA works with American companies that deliver science and technology payloads to the Moon's surface and orbit. NASA says it has awarded 17 deliveries to five companies, carrying more than 60 payloads, and that CLPS supports the Artemis campaign [6].

Intuitive Machines' IM-2 was a CLPS delivery planned to land at Mons Mouton. It touched down on March 6, 2025, more than 400 meters from its target, and ended on its side in a crater, so its instruments could not fully operate. NASA said it landed closer to the south pole than any lander before it [11]. LROC gives its position as 84.7906 S, 29.1957 E [7].

## Artemis, NASA and partners

- Artemis I, an uncrewed test of the SLS rocket and Orion spacecraft, launched on November 16, 2022 [8][9].
- Artemis II launched in April 2026 on a nearly 10-day flight around the Moon. Its crew was NASA astronauts Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover and Christina Koch, and CSA astronaut Jeremy Hansen [8]. The CSA gives the launch date as April 1, 2026 [9].
- Artemis III, planned for 2027, is now a test in low Earth orbit of docking between Orion and commercial landers. It does not land on the Moon [8].
- Artemis IV, planned for 2028, is to be the first crewed landing of the Artemis programme [8].
- NASA's Moon Base is planned as an outpost near the south pole [8].

In October 2024, when Artemis III was still the landing mission, NASA named nine candidate landing regions near the south pole: Peak near Cabeus B, Haworth, Malapert Massif, Mons Mouton Plateau, Mons Mouton, Nobile Rim 1, Nobile Rim 2, de Gerlache Rim 2 and Slater Plain. The factors used included lighting and communication with Earth [10]. Whether these regions carry over to Artemis IV is to confirm.

Canada contributes Canadarm3 to Gateway, a planned station in orbit around the Moon, and receives two astronaut flights to the Moon in return [9].
